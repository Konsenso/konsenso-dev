If you are someone who ships software, you need to learn about AI safety and security.

**The problem**: AI safety and security seem like abstract or academic concepts. 

The many different definitions and applications of the terms "AI safety" and "AI security" don't help: 

- [AI safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_safety) is "an interdisciplinary field focused on preventing accidents, misuse, or other harmful consequences arising from artificial intelligence systems. It encompasses AI alignment (which aims to ensure AI systems behave as intended), monitoring AI systems for risks, and enhancing their robustness. The field is particularly concerned with existential risks posed by advanced AI models".

  - Broadly speaking, AI safety is about preventing harms. 
  - There are many different stakeholders in "AI safety", who have different focus on what "harms" mean, what the most immediate risks of AI are, and what practices we should carry out to improve "AI safety"
  - PMs, engineers and security teams are likely most interested in the form of "AI safety" that [Konsenso](https://konsenso.dev) focuses on: product-level testing and evaluation of AI deployed by enterprises
  - A major part of the "AI safety" field is interested in 'existential risk' from questions like, 'can AI build biological weapons?' 
  - When we talk about "AI safety" here, we mean the product-level safety practices
- If you are a government or a cybersecurity vendor, AI security means different things. 

  - Governments concerned about AI-related risks to their nations have invested in AI Security institutes like the [UK AISI](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/) and many others. These organizations primarily conduct technical research and evaluations of AI frontier models. 
  - Cybersecurity and infrastructure vendors see AI security as an extension of existing cybersecurity practices. This can be offensive (let's run AI red teams to find vulnerabilities), or defensive (let's prevent malicious attacks from AI, or with AI-assisted tooling).

## Who is this for?

All the content on [Konsenso](https://konsenso.dev) is for the product manager, engineer or security team member who wants to understand the current state and best practices of AI safety and security. 

While there is plenty of high-level academic research in the AI, machine learning and natural language processing spaces, it is more challenging to find practical explanations of what any of this means for your jobs as a PM, engineer or security team member.

We wrote this guide to help point other PMs, engineers and security team members to concepts, applications and best practices that we spent years learning on the job. ## AI Safety and Security Today

In July 2026, Nvidia and other industry leaders [formed the Open Secure Alliance for AI Safety and Security](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/open-secure-ai-alliance/). 

Many enterprises still have separate AI safety and AI security teams. However, as we learn more about how safety and security overlaps, we may see more industry and academic acceptance of AI safety and security as the same discipline.

AI accelerates the pace of software development, and increases the surface area for cybersecurity attacks. Many known attacks with AI use 'safety' techniques like impersonation or low-resource language switching in order to elicit security exploits.

At Konsenso, we believe that you can't work on one without the other.
